Australian Film Awards · 1969–2025

Where Australia's Best Films Come From

Fifty-seven years of the AFI and AACTA Award for Best Film: where the winning stories are set, who made them, what kind of films they are, and where the screen money and the box office really go.

Matan Kimchi · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · October 2026

1 Half a century of winners

Since 1969 the Australian Film Institute, and from 2011 its successor the Australian Academy of Cinema and Television Arts (AACTA), has named one feature film almost every year as the country's best. The winners run from Jack and Jill: A Postscript to Bring Her Back, with joint winners in 1973 and 2014.

1.1Every winner on one reel

Changes with the decade selector

Every winner on one reel: each wedge is a winning film, reading clockwise from 1969. The decade selector filters the reel and the two maps of places (2.1 and 3.1) and highlights that decade in 4.1 and 4.2; the other charts always show all years.

1969's winner, Jack and Jill: A Postscript, has no location data on Wikidata, so the lens starts at the 1970s. 1973 and 2014 had joint winners: the second film of each pair sits on the outer ring.

1.2What the winners are about

Each cell counts winning films tagged with that genre on Wikidata. Thirty-one genres appear across the 58 winners; the eight most common have their own row and the rest are grouped as Other. Three winners have no genre recorded.

2 Where the stories are set

Of the 58 winners, 37 are set in Australia, 7 overseas, and 14 have no setting recorded on Wikidata. Outback and regional stories such as Samson and Delilah and Ten Canoes sit alongside Sydney and Melbourne dramas. The map shows where each winning film's story takes place and, where recorded, where it was shot.

3 Who made them

The directors behind Australia's best films were born on three continents. Of the 57 director credits on winning films with a located birthplace, 17 (about 30%) belong to overseas-born directors, from Hungary to the United States.

3.1Where the directors were born

Changes with the decade selector

No located director birthplaces for this decade.

Each circle is one birthplace, sized by the number of Best Film wins by directors born there. Richmond is counted with Melbourne, Camden and Paddington with Sydney, Gawler with Adelaide and Strathmore Park with Wellington. Greater Sydney and Greater Melbourne dominate the Australian panel with ten wins each; Britain supplies most of the overseas-born winners, and the Americas just one (Peekskill, New York). Where a film has two directors both are counted. The decade selector filters by award year.

3.2Australian-born directors, first half and second half

The share of wins going to Australian-born directors rose from 59% (17 of 29 wins) in 1969–1996 to 82% (23 of 28 wins) in 1997–2025. Only wins whose director has a located birthplace are counted.

3.3Directors' careers against their wins

Filmographies are counted from Wikidata, which undercounts early and television-heavy careers, so read the green dots as a floor. The chart shows the top 15 directors by films directed (ties broken alphabetically; Nadia Tass ties Bill Bennett at 15 and is dropped). Eleven directors have won Best Film more than once; Peter Weir, Fred Schepisi and Baz Luhrmann lead with three each, and five of the fifteen directors shown (Tim Burstall, Phillip Noyce, Weir, Schepisi, John Duigan) have two or more wins. George Miller and Jane Campion have one each.

4 What kind of films win

What does a Best Film look like on paper? Using the cast lists and runtimes recorded on Wikidata, the winners of the 1970s to the 2010s grew bigger: the median credited cast rose from 3 to 16. Median runtime rose from 96 minutes in the 1970s to 116 in the 2020s, while the median feature film released worldwide stayed at about 90 minutes.

4.1How many people are on screen

Changes with the decade selector

One dot per winner with a cast list on Wikidata (55 of 58). The green tick is the decade's median. The decade selector highlights one decade. The 2020s median falls back to 4.5 across six films, most likely because Wikidata cast lists for recent films are still sparse.

4.2How long they run

Changes with the decade selector

Each ochre dot is one winner's runtime (52 of 58; 6 winners have no runtime on Wikidata). The ochre line is a loess trend through the winners; the green line is the median runtime of every feature film released worldwide that year, from IMDb (40 to 300 minutes, adult titles excluded). By decade, the winners' median runtime rose from 96 minutes in the 1970s to 108.5 in the 1980s, about 101 in the 1990s and 2000s, 113 in the 2010s and 116 in the 2020s. Over the same years the world median barely moved: 90 minutes in 1970 and 90 in 2025, dipping to 86 in 2010. The decade selector highlights one decade's winners; the lines always show all years.

5 Where the money goes

Awards follow stories; money follows incentives. In 2024/25 Queensland (34.4% of the A$2.69 billion total) overtook New South Wales (30.9%) as the biggest state for drama production spending; it had led only twice before, in 1995/96 and 2002/03.

Share of the $2.69 billion spent on drama production in Australia in 2024/25, to one decimal place. * The ACT, Northern Territory and Tasmania are published only as a combined figure.

5.2Thirty years of spend

A streamgraph stacks the states around a centre line so the total width shows the whole industry's size. Values marked <0.1 in the source are drawn as 0.05. Spend is in current dollars. Western Australia and the ACT/NT/Tasmania group have no separate 2010/11 figure, so their bands narrow to zero there (no separate figure).

5.3Who is on top each year

Queensland has topped the table three times: 1995/96, 2002/03 and 2024/25. Hover a line to follow one state.

6 Do awards pay?

A Best Film trophy is not a box-office guarantee. Of the 39 winners since 1988, 29 made the top five Australian films of their release year and 11 were the year's biggest Australian film.

6.1The winners that also sold tickets

Twenty-two winners are in the all-time top 100; the number beside each bar is its rank. Not in the all-time top 100 (below about A$3.65 million): The Devil's Playground; Storm Boy; Newsfront; My Brilliant Career; Lonely Hearts; Careful, He Might Hear You; Annie's Coming Out; Bliss; Malcolm; The Year My Voice Broke; The Navigator: A Medieval Odyssey; Evil Angels; Flirting; Angel Baby; Kiss or Kill; The Interview; Somersault; Look Both Ways; Ten Canoes; Romulus, My Father; The Black Balloon; Samson and Delilah; The Babadook; Sweet Country; The Nightingale; Babyteeth; Nitram; Jack and Jill: A Postscript; Three to Go: Michael; Homesdale; Stork; 27A; Libido: The Child; Sunday Too Far Away; Proof; Bring Her Back. Box office is cumulative and in current dollars.

6.2Winner versus the year's biggest film

Year-of-release grosses from Screen Australia's top-five lists (1988–2025), so winners outside their year's top five are not shown. The thin line is the gap to the year's biggest Australian film.

About this visualisation

Data. Winning films, their settings, filming locations, directors' birthplaces, genres, cast sizes, runtimes and directors' filmographies are from Wikidata (CC0). Winners, locations, settings, directors and flows were exported on 11 October 2026; genres, facts and careers on 12 October 2026. Twelve winners missing from Wikidata's award records were identified from Wikipedia's AACTA Award for Best Film list (CC BY-SA) and then queried on Wikidata by their item IDs, like the other winners. The SPARQL queries are in data/fetch_wikidata.sh and the Screen Australia transcription in data/fetch_screen_australia.py; sources and notes are in data/README.md. Production spending, box-office rankings and yearly top fives are from Screen Australia, accessed 12 October 2026: spend by location, top Australian films of all time (top 100) and top 5 Australian films each year, plus the Drama Report 2024/25 (published 4 December 2025) for context. World feature-film runtimes are from the IMDb non-commercial datasets (title.basics, personal and non-commercial use), accessed 24 October 2026. Boundaries: Natural Earth (world) and Australian state boundaries derived from the ABS.

Method. Charts are built with Vega-Lite 5 and embedded with vega-embed; the decade selector drives a shared signal. Screen Australia tables were transcribed from the charts on its website by a script (data/fetch_screen_australia.py). The world median runtime per year is computed from IMDb's title.basics file by data/fetch_imdb_runtimes.py.
