Australian Film Awards · 1969–2025

Where Australia's Best Films Come From

Fifty-seven years of the AFI and AACTA Award for Best Film: where the winning stories are set, who made them, what kind of films they are, and where the screen money and the box office really go.

Matan Kimchi · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · October 2026

1 Half a century of winners

Since 1969 the Australian Film Institute, and from 2011 its successor the Australian Academy of Cinema and Television Arts (AACTA), has named one feature film almost every year as the country's best. The winners run from Jack and Jill: A Postscript to Bring Her Back, with joint winners in 1973 and 2014.

1.1Every winner on one reel

Changes with the decade selector

Every winner on one reel: each wedge is a winning film, reading clockwise from 1969. The decade selector filters the reel and the two maps of places (2.1 and 3.1) and highlights that decade in 4.2; the other charts always show all years.

1969's winner, Jack and Jill: A Postscript, has no location data on Wikidata, so the lens starts at the 1970s. 1973 and 2014 had joint winners: the second film of each pair sits on the outer ring.

2 Where the stories are set

Of the 58 winners, 37 are set in Australia, 7 overseas, and 14 have no setting recorded on Wikidata. Outback and regional stories such as Samson and Delilah and Ten Canoes sit alongside Sydney and Melbourne dramas. The map shows where each winning film's story takes place and, where recorded, where it was shot.

2.1Where the winning stories are set and shot

Each ochre spike counts the winning films whose story is set at that place; each green spike counts the winners shot there. Where Wikidata records only a whole state, the spike stands at the state's centre and is drawn paler. Studio lots are counted with their city (Disney Studios Australia with Sydney, Docklands Studios with Melbourne). Only places recorded on Wikidata are shown.

2.2Set in Australia, set overseas, or no setting data

One square per winning film. Ochre squares are set in Australia, green squares overseas, dashed outlines have no story setting recorded on Wikidata. Read left to right, top to bottom; the three groups are ordered by year.

3 Who made them

The directors behind Australia's best films were born on three continents. Of the 57 director credits on winning films with a located birthplace, 17 (about 30%) belong to overseas-born directors, from Hungary to the United States.

3.1Where the directors were born

Changes with the decade selector

No located director birthplaces for this decade.

A dot map: each dot is one Best Film win, packed in a small grid at the winning director's birthplace; where a film has two directors both are counted. Richmond is merged into Greater Melbourne, Camden and Paddington into Greater Sydney, Gawler into Greater Adelaide and Strathmore Park into Wellington, with the dots placed at the main city. Greater Sydney and Greater Melbourne lead the Australian panel with ten wins each; Britain supplies most of the overseas-born winners, and the Americas just one (Peekskill, New York). The decade selector filters by award year.

3.2Australian-born directors, first half and second half

The share of wins going to Australian-born directors rose from 59% (17 of 29 wins) in 1969–1996 to 82% (23 of 28 wins) in 1997–2025. Only wins whose director has a located birthplace are counted.

3.3Directors' careers against their wins

Filmographies are counted from Wikidata, which undercounts early and television-heavy careers, so read the green dots as a floor. The chart shows the top 15 winning directors by films directed (ties broken alphabetically; Nadia Tass ties Bill Bennett at 15 and is dropped). Wins sit on their own 0–3 scale on the right so they are not squashed against film counts. Eleven directors have won Best Film more than once, but only five of them are among the fifteen most prolific shown here (Tim Burstall, Phillip Noyce, Peter Weir, Fred Schepisi and John Duigan); the other ten won once, among them Bruce Beresford with 37 films. Baz Luhrmann matches Weir and Schepisi's three wins from only nine films, so he is not shown.

4 What kind of films win

What does a Best Film look like on paper? Drama is the genre Wikidata records most often for the winners, and it has won in every decade. The winners have also grown longer: their middle runtime rose from 96 minutes in the 1970s to 116 in the 2020s. Yet the Oscar winners for Best Picture ran longer still in every decade.

4.1What the winners are about

Each cell counts winning films tagged with that genre on Wikidata. Thirty-one genres appear across the 58 winners; the eight most common have their own row and the rest are grouped as Other. Three winners have no genre recorded.

4.2How long they run

Changes with the decade selector

Each ochre dot is one Australian winner's runtime (52 of 58; 6 winners have no runtime on Wikidata). Each green dot is the Oscar Best Picture winner for the same year of films (57 winners, 1969–2025, all with a runtime on Wikidata). Each line is a smoothed average of the dots of its colour. The median is the middle value: half the films run shorter, half longer. By decade, the Australian median rose from 96 minutes in the 1970s to 108.5 in the 1980s, about 101 in the 1990s and 2000s, 113 in the 2010s and 116 in the 2020s; the Oscar median was 132.5, 130, 152, 131.5, 121.5 and 139 minutes. The gap was smallest in the 2010s, at 8.5 minutes. The decade selector highlights one decade's dots in both colours; the lines always show all years.

5 Where the money goes

Awards follow stories; money follows incentives. In 2024/25 Queensland (34.4% of the A$2.69 billion total) overtook New South Wales (30.9%) as the biggest state for drama production spending; it had led only twice before, in 1995/96 and 2002/03.

5.1Queensland overtook New South Wales in 2024/25

Share of the $2.69 billion spent on drama production in Australia in 2024/25, to one decimal place. The ACT, the Northern Territory and Tasmania are published only as one combined figure (0.7% of spend), so the three share one neutral fill with a dashed outline instead of being shaded as 0.7% each.

5.2Thirty years of spend

A streamgraph stacks the states around a centre line so the total width shows the whole industry's size. Values marked <0.1 in the source are drawn as 0.05. Spend is in current dollars. Western Australia and the ACT/NT/Tasmania group have no separate 2010/11 figure, so their bands narrow to zero there (no separate figure).

5.3Who is on top each year

Queensland has topped the table three times: 1995/96, 2002/03 and 2024/25. Hover a line to follow one state.

5.4Stories versus money

Ochre: each state's share of the winners set there. Twenty-one winners have a story setting located in Australia; Lion is set in both Tasmania and Victoria and counts once in each, giving 22 in all. Settings recorded only as "Australia" are left out. Green: each state's share of drama production spend, averaged over the 30 years 1995/96 to 2024/25. Screen Australia publishes the ACT, Tasmania and the Northern Territory as one figure, so settings there are grouped the same way. The stories and the money part ways most in Tasmania and the territories (22.7% of the stories, 0.9% of the spend) and in Queensland (4.5% against 19.9%); New South Wales leads both.

6 Do awards pay?

A Best Film trophy is not a box-office guarantee. Of the 39 winners since 1988, 29 made the top five Australian films of their release year and 11 were the year's biggest Australian film.

6.1The winners that also sold tickets

Twenty-two winners are in the all-time top 100; the number beside each bar is its rank. 36 winners earned less than the top-100 cut-off (about A$3.65 million), including Samson and Delilah, The Babadook and Nitram; hover a bar for its gross. Box office is cumulative and in current dollars.

6.2Winner versus the year's biggest film

Year-of-release grosses from Screen Australia's top-five lists (1988–2025), so winners outside their year's top five are not shown. The thin line is the gap to the year's biggest Australian film.

About this visualisation

Data. Winning films, their settings, filming locations, directors' birthplaces, genres, cast sizes, runtimes and directors' filmographies are from Wikidata (CC0). Winners, locations, settings, directors and flows were exported on 11 October 2026; genres, facts and careers on 12 October 2026. Twelve winners missing from Wikidata's award records were identified from Wikipedia's AACTA Award for Best Film list (CC BY-SA) and then queried on Wikidata by their item IDs, like the other winners. The SPARQL queries are in data/fetch_wikidata.sh and the Screen Australia transcription in data/fetch_screen_australia.py; sources and notes are in data/README.md. Production spending, box-office rankings and yearly top fives are from Screen Australia, accessed 12 October 2026: spend by location, top Australian films of all time (top 100) and top 5 Australian films each year, plus the Drama Report 2024/25 (published 4 December 2025) for context. World feature-film runtimes are from the IMDb non-commercial datasets (title.basics, personal and non-commercial use), accessed 24 October 2026. Boundaries: Natural Earth (world) and Australian state boundaries derived from the ABS.

Method. Charts are built with Vega-Lite 5 and embedded with vega-embed; the decade selector drives a shared signal. Screen Australia tables were transcribed from the charts on its website by a script (data/fetch_screen_australia.py). The world median runtime per year is computed from IMDb's title.basics file by data/fetch_imdb_runtimes.py.

It was used to write and debug the Vega-Lite chart specifications, the page's HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and the data-collection scripts (the Wikidata SPARQL queries and the Screen Australia transcription script); to draft and proofread the narrative text and captions; and to brainstorm and mock up alternative chart designs after the peer critique. The topic, the story, the choice of data sources and charts, the colour scheme, the critique responses and the hand-drawn sketch are my own decisions, and I checked every figure quoted on this page against the data files in the repository. No AI-generated content is presented as data.
