Australian Film Awards · 1969–2025

Where Australia's Best Films Come From

More than fifty years of the AFI and AACTA Award for Best Film, mapped: where the winning stories are set, who made them, and where the screen money really goes.

Matan Kimchi · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · October 2026

1 Half a century of winners

Since 1969 the Australian Film Institute, and from 2011 its successor the Australian Academy of Cinema and Television Arts (AACTA), has named one feature film almost every year as the country's best. The winners run from Jack and Jill: A Postscript to Bring Her Back, with joint winners in 1973 and 2014.

Every winner on one reel: each wedge is a winning film, reading clockwise from 1969. The decade selector above filters the reel, the settings map and the flow map; the squares and the spending map always show all years.

2 Where the stories are set

Most winners are set in Australia, but rarely in the same Australia. Outback and regional stories such as Samson and Delilah and Ten Canoes sit alongside Sydney and Melbourne dramas. The map shows where each winning film's story takes place and, where recorded, where it was shot.

Filled circles mark story settings; hollow rings mark filming locations. Where Wikidata records only a whole state as the setting, the circle sits at the state's centre. Only places recorded on Wikidata are shown.

One square per winning film. The 12 winners added from Wikipedia were not looked up on Wikidata, so they are counted as No setting data.

3 Who made them

The directors behind Australia's best films were born on three continents. Roughly a third were born overseas, from Hungary to the United States, which says something about who gets to tell Australian stories.

Each line runs from a director's birthplace to the place their winning film is set. Gold lines start in Australia; cream lines start overseas. Films whose setting is recorded only as a whole country have no line.

4 Where the money goes

Awards follow stories; money follows incentives. In 2024/25 Queensland became the biggest state for drama production spending for the first time, overtaking New South Wales, as foreign productions chased the federal Location Offset.

Share of the $2.7 billion spent on drama production in Australia, 2024/25. The ACT, Northern Territory and Tasmania are only published as a combined 1%.

About this visualisation

Data. Winning films, their settings, filming locations and directors' birthplaces are from Wikidata (CC0), exported 11 October 2026. Twelve winners missing from Wikidata's award records were identified from Wikipedia's AACTA Award for Best Film list (CC BY-SA); their directors were read from their Wikidata items, but their settings were not looked up. The exact queries are in data/README.md. Production spending is from Screen Australia, Drama Report 2024/25 (published 4 December 2025). Boundaries: Natural Earth (world) and Australian state boundaries derived from the ABS.

Method. Charts are built with Vega-Lite. Generative AI (Claude) was used to draft chart code and to check the grammar of this text; all design decisions and the hand-drawn sketch are the author's.
